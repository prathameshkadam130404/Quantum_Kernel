\medskip
\textbf{Contribution.}
The paper introduces the Bell-decomposition family of two-qubit quantum-kernel feature maps, unifying the singlet-only Self-Regulating Quantum Feature Map (SRQFM) and the four-Bell-sector Bell-Spectrum Coupling Map (BSCM) under an adjustable prior.  The family sits inside the canonical two-body Pauli-rotation feature-map framework of Suzuki \emph{et al.}\ (QMI 2020); within that framework, we prove two closed-form structural identities specific to the Bell-projector construction --- a tight prior-independent bound $|\alpha|\le 1/2$ on every per-pair Pauli coefficient, and the identity $\alpha_{ZZ}\equiv 0$ for the uniform prior (Theorem 1, full proofs in the Appendix, numerically unit-tested to $10^{-8}$).  The proof technique is elementary substitution of the standard two-qubit Bell--Pauli identities into the construction; the contribution is the construction itself --- a usable closed-form quantum-kernel design device --- and the design-relevant identities it admits.

\medskip
\textbf{Empirical results.}
At 16 qubits and $N=10{,}000$ on So2Sat LCZ42 (17 classes) and EuroSAT (10 classes), the Bell-decomposition projected kernels add $+0.167$--$+0.170$ macro-F1 over the canonical commuting $ZZ$-PQK on So2Sat and $+0.097$--$+0.108$ on EuroSAT (Holm-corrected Wilcoxon $p = 0.0078$; complementary exact McNemar tests give $p \ll 10^{-100}$ on both datasets).  The same architectural gap holds with positive sign on every split across four UCI tabular datasets (largest gap $+0.646$ accuracy on the 26-class Letter Recognition task), on a transverse-field Ising-model phase-classification benchmark (E49), on learned AE-16 deep features (E42), and under density-matrix simulation with realistic depolarising noise at $n=8$ (E48, gap survives at $+0.133$--$+0.136$, Wilcoxon $p = 6.1\times 10^{-5}$).  Two complementary measurements (a 14-qubit BSCM build and a 16-qubit depth scan) reproduce the Thanasilp \emph{et al.}\ concentration signature on real multi-class data.

\medskip
\textbf{Honest negatives.}
We make no claim of quantum advantage: at every scale tested, tuned classical RBF-SVM ties or exceeds the best Bell-decomposition kernel, consistent with recent theory on bandwidth-tuning convergence (Canatar et al.; Fl{\'o}rez-Ablan, Roth and Schnabel) and prior empirical benchmarks (Bowles et al.; Schnabel and Roth, QMI 2025).  The within-family architectural gap is reported as the main contribution: it is a design lesson for quantum-kernel architects --- single-axis commuting $ZZ$ generators carry a structural pathology that the broader non-commuting Pauli generator family does not --- and is independent of whether quantum-vs-classical advantage is reached on a given task.

\medskip
\textbf{Fit with QMI.}
The paper is directly thematically aligned with the journal: it places its theoretical contribution within Suzuki \emph{et al.}'s two-body Pauli-rotation feature-map framework (QMI 2020), it extends the benchmarking framework of Schnabel \& Roth (QMI 2025) to a larger fixed-protocol multi-class Earth-observation setting, and it directly engages with the bandwidth-tuning convergence picture established by Fl{\'o}rez-Ablan, Roth and Schnabel (Quantum Sci.\ Tech.\ 2025).  The combination of $n = 16$ qubits, $N = 10{,}000$, $K\in\{10, 17\}$ classes, and Holm-corrected paired significance tests on real remote-sensing data is, to our knowledge, the largest such benchmark in the QML kernel literature to date.
